Simple infusions

Traditional home preparations. They are comfort drinks, not treatments.

Fresh mint tea

A handful of leaves, 250 ml hot water, steep 5 minutes. Often used after meals.

Ginger–tulsi kadha

Sliced ginger and 8–10 tulsi leaves simmered in water for 5–10 minutes. A common household drink in India. Add jaggery or honey if you like.

Thyme and honey

A few sprigs steeped 10 minutes with a spoon of honey. Traditionally taken for a scratchy throat. Do not give honey to children under 1 year.

Rosemary water

One sprig steeped in hot water for 10 minutes. Mild, resinous and best in small quantities.

Grow your own

Easy starters for a sunny kitchen window or balcony.

Easiest

  • Mint: grow in its own pot, it spreads
  • Tulsi: 4–6 hours of sun, pinch flower buds
  • Curry leaf: slow but long-lived

Need care

  • Basil: warm, regular water, harvest the tips
  • Coriander: sow fresh every 3–4 weeks
  • Dill: sow directly, dislikes transplanting

Harvest rules

  • Cut in the morning
  • Never take more than a third at once
  • Rosemary and thyme prefer drier soil

Safety and interactions

Blood thinners

Parsley, basil and coriander are rich in vitamin K. If you take warfarin, keep your intake steady and ask your doctor.

Pregnancy and breastfeeding

Food amounts are generally fine. Concentrated teas, extracts and supplements of herbs such as sage, parsley and tulsi need medical advice first.

Medicines and surgery

Garlic and ginger in large doses may affect bleeding. Mention herbal products to your doctor before surgery.

Allergies

Herbs in the mint and carrot families can cause reactions in some people. Stop if you notice itching or swelling.
